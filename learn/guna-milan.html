Guna milan: how the 36 points are counted

Each of the eight kootas, how it is scored, and a full worked example.

Guna milan is the 36-point score from comparing two Moons. It adds eight kootas worth 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 points. Each koota has its own simple rule, so you can check any score by hand.
In real life

For example, Kavya’s uncle in Nagpur adds the pandit’s printed column himself, 1, 2, 3, 1, 5, 6, 0, 8, and gets 26. Her grandmother then asks about the single zero, which is a better question than the total.

What guna milan means

Guna means a quality or a point and milan means meeting, so guna milan is “the meeting of qualities”. It is the scoring half of kundli matching, and another name for the Ashtakoota system. The 36 points come from eight kootas worth 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 points. The weights rise on purpose: the classical view is that the later kootas, such as Bhakoot and Nadi, touch deeper parts of married life than the early ones.

What is compared

Only the Moon is used. For each person the system takes the Moon sign (rashi) and the nakshatra with its pada, and from those reads the Moon sign’s ruling planet, Varna, Vashya, Gana, Nadi and Yoni. A few kootas treat the boy and the girl differently, such as Varna and Gana, so the order of the two charts matters. The free tool asks for both in separate forms for that reason.

Learn the basics: Moon sign and nakshatra

Kundli matching starts from the Moon, not the Sun. Your Moon sign (rashi) is the zodiac sign the Moon was in when you were born. Your nakshatra (birth star) is the one of 27 equal slices of the sky, each 13°20' wide, that the Moon was crossing, and each nakshatra has four quarters called padas. Vedic astrology treats the Moon as the mind, meaning how you feel, react and settle, which is why it carries the match. A koota is one of the eight scored categories, a dosha is a flagged imbalance in a chart, a dasha is a planetary period that runs for years, and the Navamsa (D9) is the nine-part chart classically used to read marriage. You can find your Moon sign and nakshatra in the free kundli.

How each koota is counted

1. Varna (1 point)

Each Moon sign belongs to one of four classical groups: Brahmin (Cancer, Scorpio, Pisces), Kshatriya (Aries, Leo, Sagittarius), Vaishya (Taurus, Virgo, Capricorn) or Shudra (Gemini, Libra, Aquarius). The point is given when the boy’s group is equal to or higher than the girl’s. Today it is read less as social rank and more as a sign of temperament and how much each expects the other to defer.

2. Vashya (2 points)

Signs are grouped by a symbolic creature type: four-legged, human, water, wild and insect. Same group scores 2 and a few mixed pairs score 1 or half, while the pairs that clash most score 0. It describes who naturally gives way in a disagreement.

3. Tara (3 points)

Count from the girl’s nakshatra to the boy’s and divide by 9. If the remainder is 3, 5 or 7 (the positions called Vipat, Pratyak and Vadha) that direction scores 0, otherwise 1.5. Then count the other way. The two halves add to 0, 1.5 or 3.

4. Yoni (4 points)

Each nakshatra has an animal. The same animal scores 4, friendly animals 3, neutral 2, unfriendly 1 and natural enemies 0. See yoni matching.

5. Graha Maitri (5 points)

This compares the planets ruling the two Moon signs, using the classical table of planetary friendships. Two friends or the same ruler score 5. A friend and a neutral score 4, two neutrals 3, a friend and an enemy 1, a neutral and an enemy half a point, and two enemies 0. It describes day-to-day mental fit.

6. Gana (6 points)

Every nakshatra is Deva (gentle), Manushya (practical) or Rakshasa (intense and forceful). Same type scores 6, and so does a Manushya boy with a Deva girl. A Deva boy with a Manushya girl scores 5 and with a Rakshasa girl 1. A Manushya boy with a Rakshasa girl, or a Rakshasa boy with a Deva or Manushya girl, scores 0. Read the Gana guide.

7. Bhakoot (7 points)

Count the distance between the two Moon signs in both directions. If the pair is 6/8, 5/9 or 2/12 the score is 0, otherwise 7. See Bhakoot dosha.

8. Nadi (8 points)

Every nakshatra belongs to one of three Nadis. The same Nadi scores 0 and different Nadis score 8. See Nadi dosha.

A worked example

Take Aarav, whose Moon is in Rohini nakshatra in Taurus, and Diya, whose Moon is in Hasta nakshatra in Virgo.

Three kootas lost points. Vashya lost 1 and Yoni lost 3, so their instinctive and physical rhythms differ a little. Bhakoot lost all 7 because Taurus to Virgo is a 5/9 pair. Venus and Mercury, the rulers of those two signs, are friends, so many pandits treat that Bhakoot loss as cancelled, which would lift the effective picture well above the 25 on paper. The sensible reading is a strong score with one flagged koota worth a conversation.

Why scores differ between websites

If you try three websites you may see three totals. That is normal. Half points in Vashya and Graha Maitri are handled differently in different books, some sites ignore cancellations and others apply them, and a few mix in the 10-koota South Indian system. The eight-koota framework is shared, but the fine print is not. Use the total as a rough guide and the individual kootas as the real information.

What the total cannot do

A guna total says nothing about the two people’s dasha periods, the 7th house, the Navamsa chart or how the families get on. It also gives the same score for a couple at 22 and a couple at 42, though their lives are in very different places. Treat it as the first filter and read the full charts before deciding.

Frequently asked questions

How many gunas out of 36 is good?

Scores of 25 and above are usually called good and 18 to 24 acceptable. Below 18 is weak on paper, but which kootas lost the points matters more than the total.

Which koota is the most important?

Nadi carries the most points (8), followed by Bhakoot (7). Many jyotishis feel Graha Maitri, the mental friendship, shapes daily life most, even though it is worth only 5.

Why does my guna milan score differ between websites?

Different books use slightly different tables for the smaller kootas, and some sites apply dosha cancellations while others do not. The framework is the same, so small differences of a point or two are normal.

Is guna milan the same as kundli matching?

Guna milan is the scoring part of kundli matching. A full match also checks Manglik dosha and, in a good reading, the Navamsa chart, the 7th house and the dasha periods.

Do both people need the same nakshatra?

No. Two people in the same nakshatra score well on some kootas and poorly on Nadi, so the pairing is judged koota by koota rather than by sameness.
