Nadi dosha: meaning, cancellation and the honest view

The 8-point koota that makes families go quiet, and what it actually says.

Nadi dosha is when both people fall in the same one of three Nadis, so the 8-point Nadi koota scores 0. About one random couple in three shares a Nadi. Classical texts cancel it in several cases, and many jyotishis weigh it against the full chart.
In real life

For example, at a rishta meeting in Rewa, Kabir’s mother hears “same Nadi, zero out of eight” and the room goes quiet. His aunt asks the useful question: which nakshatras are they, and are the padas different?

What is Nadi?

Nadi literally means a channel or pulse. In kundli matching it is one of three groups into which the 27 nakshatras are sorted, named after the three Ayurvedic body types: Adi (also called Adya, linked to Vata or wind), Madhya (Pitta or heat) and Antya (Kapha or steadiness). The idea is that each person has a dominant constitution, and the Nadi koota compares the two.

NadiIts nine nakshatras
Adi (Adya, Vata)Ashwini, Ardra, Punarvasu, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Jyeshtha, Mula, Shatabhisha, Purva Bhadrapada
Madhya (Pitta)Bharani, Mrigashira, Pushya, Purva Phalguni, Chitra, Anuradha, Purva Ashadha, Dhanishta, Uttara Bhadrapada
Antya (Kapha)Krittika, Rohini, Ashlesha, Magha, Swati, Vishakha, Uttara Ashadha, Shravana, Revati

The nakshatras run in a zigzag. Ashwini is Adi, Bharani is Madhya, Krittika is Antya, then Rohini is Antya again and the pattern turns back: Mrigashira Madhya, Ardra Adi, and so on. You can find your nakshatra free in the kundli tool.

Learn the basics: Moon sign and nakshatra

Kundli matching starts from the Moon, not the Sun. Your Moon sign (rashi) is the zodiac sign the Moon was in when you were born. Your nakshatra (birth star) is the one of 27 equal slices of the sky, each 13°20' wide, that the Moon was crossing, and each nakshatra has four quarters called padas. Vedic astrology treats the Moon as the mind, meaning how you feel, react and settle, which is why it carries the match. A koota is one of the eight scored categories, a dosha is a flagged imbalance in a chart, a dasha is a planetary period that runs for years, and the Navamsa (D9) is the nine-part chart classically used to read marriage. What is Nadi dosha?

If the two people have the same Nadi, the koota scores 0 out of 8. Different Nadis score the full 8. Because Nadi is the heaviest koota, losing it costs more than a fifth of the whole 36. Nine nakshatras in each of three groups means that about one random couple in three shares a Nadi, so this is common, not rare.

Why is it weighted so heavily?

The traditional reasoning is that Nadi stands for body constitution, and that two people with the same dominant constitution may pass on an unbalanced one, affecting the health of children. In an older world with little medical testing, that was a cautious rule of thumb. One further explanation sometimes offered is that it discouraged marriage between close relatives, who often share a nakshatra, though that is a later reading rather than a classical statement.

Honestly, there is no scientific evidence that Nadi predicts children’s health. If that is the worry, a doctor’s carrier screening, such as a thalassemia test and the blood group and Rh check, tells you far more than any koota can. The rule survives because it is old, because the weight is hard-coded in every printed chart, and because families prefer to be careful.

When Nadi dosha is cancelled

Classical sources and common practice allow the dosha to be treated as cancelled in these cases:

  • Same nakshatra, different pada. Both Moons are in the same nakshatra but in different quarters. Krittika’s first pada is in Aries and the other three are in Taurus, so two people can share Krittika with different padas.
  • Same Moon sign, different nakshatra. Krittika and Rohini both fall in Taurus and both are Antya, but they are different nakshatras in the same sign.

Some texts add further exceptions, and they do not agree with each other, so a pandit may apply one rule that another ignores. When the Nadi is the same, the nakshatras differ and the signs differ as well, the Pushyara tool says plainly that the dosha stands and leaves the decision to a fuller reading. It does not apply cancellations that the texts do not clearly support.

The modern debate

Practising jyotishis now fall into roughly three camps. Some still treat same-Nadi as a hard stop, especially for the health of children. Others treat it as a flag, check the Navamsa chart and the 5th house (children) in both kundlis, and decide from there. A third group says the rule has no basis in the planets at all and ignores it. All three are sincere, and none has evidence that settles it.

The chart-based view that Pushyara follows is the second one. Nadi tells you to look at the 5th house, its lord and Jupiter, the planet of children, in each chart, and at the timing of the two dasha periods. Those are specific, checkable parts of a chart. A zero in one koota is not.

If your match shows same Nadi

  1. Check the nakshatras and padas. If they differ in the ways above, the dosha is treated as cancelled.
  2. Look at the other seven kootas. A match that is strong elsewhere has not become weak because of one zero.
  3. Ask for the full-chart reading with the 5th house and the dasha timing, which gives real dates and not a single yes or no.
  4. Speak to your doctor about carrier screening if children’s health is the concern.

Frequently asked questions

Is Nadi dosha serious?

Traditionally it is the heaviest dosha because it carries 8 of the 36 points. In practice many jyotishis treat it as a prompt to check the 5th house and the dasha timing, not as a ban.

What does same Nadi mean in kundli matching?

It means both Moons fall in nakshatras from the same one of the three groups, Adi, Madhya or Antya, so the Nadi koota scores 0 out of 8.

How common is Nadi dosha?

About one couple in three. Each Nadi holds nine of the 27 nakshatras, so a random pair shares a Nadi a third of the time.

Is Nadi dosha cancelled if the padas are different?

Yes, in the common rule: if both Moons are in the same nakshatra but different padas, the dosha is treated as cancelled. The same applies when the nakshatras differ but the Moon sign is the same.

Does Nadi dosha mean the couple cannot have healthy children?

No. There is no evidence that Nadi predicts that. Medical carrier screening is the reliable way to learn about health risks for children.
