Kundli matching: how it works and what it can tell you

The eight-part check families run before a rishta, explained without fear.

Kundli matching (kundli milan) compares the Moon in two birth charts across eight areas called kootas, scored out of 36. The traditional minimum is 18. It is a useful first filter, but it reads only the Moon, so the full charts say more.
In real life

For example, Meera’s parents in Jabalpur get a rishta for her, and the pandit says “twenty-two gun”. Her mother is relieved, and her father asks which kootas lost the missing fourteen points, which is the question that makes the number useful.

What is kundli matching?

Kundli matching, also called kundli milan, gun milan or horoscope matching, is the practice of comparing two birth charts (kundlis) before a marriage. Across north and central India the standard method is Ashtakoota milan. Ashta means eight and a koota is a category, so it is a check of eight categories, each carrying a fixed number of points that add up to 36. Each point is called a guna, which is why people say “twenty-two gun”.

The method compares the Moon in the two charts: its sign and the nakshatra it was crossing. The Moon is used because Vedic astrology reads it as the mind, meaning temperament, mood and how a person responds to the people close to them. Two people can have very different careers and still get along if their Moons are in tune, which is the idea behind the whole system.

Learn the basics: Moon sign and nakshatra

Kundli matching starts from the Moon, not the Sun. Your Moon sign (rashi) is the zodiac sign the Moon was in when you were born. Your nakshatra (birth star) is the one of 27 equal slices of the sky, each 13°20' wide, that the Moon was crossing, and each nakshatra has four quarters called padas. Vedic astrology treats the Moon as the mind, meaning how you feel, react and settle, which is why it carries the match. A koota is one of the eight scored categories, a dosha is a flagged imbalance in a chart, a dasha is a planetary period that runs for years, and the Navamsa (D9) is the nine-part chart classically used to read marriage. Notice the weighting. Nadi and Bhakoot together carry 15 of the 36 points, which is why those two get most of the worry. Each has its own page: Nadi dosha and Bhakoot dosha. A dosha here simply means a koota that scored zero because a classical rule was triggered. Yoni and Gana are the next two most discussed, and the guna milan guide shows how every koota is counted.

What does 18 out of 36 mean?

The traditional minimum is 18, which is exactly half. Most pandits group scores roughly like this: below 18 is weak on paper, 18 to 24 is acceptable, 25 to 32 is good and above 32 is rare. These bands are convention, not law, and two jyotishis may not use the same cut-offs.

It helps to be clear about what the number is. A score of 18 does not mean an 18-in-36 chance that the marriage works. It counts how many of eight categories line up when you compare two Moons. A couple can score 31 and still struggle because of something no koota measures, and a couple on 16 can build a calm home. The useful habit is to look at which kootas lost points, not only the total, and the free tool shows each one.

Manglik: the other check

Separate from the 36 points is the Manglik check. A chart is called Manglik when Mars (Mangal) sits in the 1st, 2nd, 4th, 7th, 8th or 12th house, counted from the ascendant and often also from the Moon and Venus. Mars is the planet of heat and force, so the old reading is that it can bring sharpness into a partnership. The rule catches a large share of all charts, many classical cancellations exist, and two Manglik charts together are traditionally considered to cancel each other. What kundli matching cannot tell you

Guna milan reads one planet, the Moon, in two charts. It cannot see whether the two people respect each other, how their families will adjust, or what each person wants from life. Inside the charts themselves it also leaves out:

  • The Navamsa (D9) chart, the nine-part chart classically used for marriage.
  • The 7th house and its lord, the part of each chart that describes partnership.
  • Dasha timing: which planetary period each person is in, and whether the two periods help or strain each other in the coming years.

No chart can say that a marriage will succeed or fail. What a full reading can do is describe patterns and show dates when a stretch is easier or heavier for each person, so you go in informed.

How to use a matching score sensibly

  1. Get both birth times as exactly as you can. Ask the hospital record or the older family members.
  2. Run the free matching tool.
  3. Look at which kootas lost points. A zero in Varna costs one point, a zero in Nadi costs eight.
  4. If the score is under 18 or a dosha appears, check the cancellation rules, then ask for a full-chart reading rather than deciding on the number.
  5. Then talk to each other. A match score is a conversation starter, never a substitute for one.

Frequently asked questions

The questions below are the ones families ask most.

What is kundli matching?

Kundli matching compares two birth charts before marriage. The standard method, Ashtakoota milan, scores the two Moons across eight categories for a total of 36 points.

How many gunas are needed for marriage?

The traditional minimum is 18 out of 36. Between 18 and 24 is usually called acceptable, 25 to 32 good and above 32 very good. The number is a convention, and the full charts matter more.

Can I do kundli matching without an exact birth time?

Mostly yes. The Moon changes sign about every two and a half days and nakshatra about every day, so an error of an hour or two rarely changes the result unless the Moon was close to a boundary. The Manglik check from the ascendant does need an accurate time.

Does kundli matching apply to a love marriage?

The method works for any two people, whether the match was arranged or chosen. Many couples use it to understand where they differ, rather than to decide whether to marry.

Is a low guna score a reason to say no?

Not by itself. A low score tells you which of eight areas to look at more closely. Check the cancellations, the Navamsa chart, the 7th house and the dasha timing before deciding.
