Yoni matching: the 14 animals and the 4-point koota

What the animal in your kundli means, and what the score actually says.

Yoni koota gives every nakshatra one of 14 animals and scores the pair out of 4. The same animal scores 4 and natural enemies score 0. It describes physical and instinctive ease, not attraction or character.
In real life

For example, at a family dinner in Bhopal, Tanvi’s chart says Serpent and her fiancé’s says Mongoose, and her cousin laughs about snake and mongoose. The pandit smiles and says the joke is the point: it only shows a topic the two should talk about openly.

What is Yoni?

Yoni here means a type of creature, one of the 14 animals the old system assigns to the 27 nakshatras. The Yoni koota is the fourth of the eight kootas in guna milan and carries 4 points. It describes physical and instinctive compatibility: how comfortable the two people’s natural rhythms are with each other, including in closeness. It does not describe looks, attraction or how fond they are of each other.

The animals come from an old habit of describing instincts through creatures people knew well: a horse is quick and restless, an elephant steady and heavy, a cat private and particular, a deer gentle and watchful. Each nakshatra carries one of these as a shorthand for its natural pace and way of reacting.

Learn the basics: Moon sign and nakshatra

Kundli matching starts from the Moon, not the Sun. Your Moon sign (rashi) is the zodiac sign the Moon was in when you were born. Your nakshatra (birth star) is the one of 27 equal slices of the sky, each 13°20' wide, that the Moon was crossing, and each nakshatra has four quarters called padas. Vedic astrology treats the Moon as the mind, meaning how you feel, react and settle, which is why it carries the match. A koota is one of the eight scored categories, a dosha is a flagged imbalance in a chart, a dasha is a planetary period that runs for years, and the Navamsa (D9) is the nine-part chart classically used to read marriage. You can find your Moon sign and nakshatra in the free kundli.

How the score works

The two animals are compared on a graded scale:

  • Same animal: 4 points.
  • Friendly animals: 3 points.
  • Neutral animals: 2 points.
  • Unfriendly animals: 1 point.
  • Natural enemies: 0 points.

There are seven pairs of natural enemies, and each animal has exactly one:

The Pushyara tool uses a full 14 by 14 table with scores from 0 to 4. Some printed books give slightly different values for the in-between pairs, but the same-animal and enemy pairs agree everywhere, which is why totals can differ a little between websites.

What the score actually describes

Nobody expects a Serpent person to behave like a snake. The animals are a way of grouping instincts: pace, appetite, need for space, how a person reacts when pushed. Yoni asks whether two such instincts sit easily side by side. A high score suggests the two settle into a shared rhythm with little effort. A low score only says that the rhythms differ, so the two may need to talk about closeness, privacy and timing more openly than other couples.

In real life: a low yoni score

For example, Imran likes quiet evenings at home and his wife Sana likes meeting friends most nights. Their Yoni score is 1 out of 4, and the first thing they learn is to plan the week so each gets some of both.

How common is a zero?

Only about one pair in fourteen, roughly 7 percent of random couples, falls on an enemy pair. A zero in Yoni costs 4 of the 36 points, much less than Nadi (8) or Bhakoot (7), so it rarely decides a match on its own.

Male and female nakshatras

Each nakshatra is also tagged male or female. In the old tables the ideal Yoni match is a male and a female of the same animal, for example Ashwini with Shatabhisha, both Horse. Many modern charts, including the one used here, score by the animal pair alone. If the tags matter to your family’s pandit, it is a fair question to ask which table he is using.

Where Yoni sits in the bigger picture

Four points out of 36 is just over 11 percent of the score. Yoni says nothing about the 7th house, Venus, the Navamsa chart or the dasha timing, which describe partnership far more directly. Treat it as the part of the match that invites an honest conversation, not as a verdict.

  1. Find the nakshatra of each person in the kundli.
  2. Read the animals from the table above.
  3. Check the pair against the enemy list.
  4. If the score is low, look at the other kootas and then at the full charts.

Frequently asked questions

What is Yoni in kundli matching?

Yoni is the animal assigned to each nakshatra. The Yoni koota compares the two animals and scores the pair out of 4 points for physical and instinctive compatibility.

Which Yoni animals are enemies?

Horse and Buffalo, Elephant and Lion, Sheep and Monkey, Serpent and Mongoose, Dog and Deer, Cat and Rat, and Cow and Tiger.

How do I find my Yoni?

First find your birth nakshatra from your date, time and place of birth, for example in the free kundli, then read your animal from the table on this page.

Does a zero in Yoni mean the marriage will not work?

No. It means the two natural rhythms differ and the couple may need more open conversation about closeness. It costs 4 of the 36 points and can be read alongside the rest of the match.

Is Yoni only about physical compatibility?

It is mostly about instinctive and physical ease, including closeness, but it is only one of eight kootas and says little about character, values or the long term.
