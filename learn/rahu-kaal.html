Rahu kaal explained

A daily window of about 80 minutes, worked out from sunrise

Rahu kaal is one eighth of the daylight hours, and which eighth depends on the weekday. It is a daily timing custom many Indian families use to avoid starting new things. It is the same for everyone in a city and says nothing about your own chart.

Key points

  • Daylight from sunrise to sunset is cut into eight equal parts
  • The weekday decides which part is Rahu kaal
  • Your city's sunrise moves the window, so a chart for another city can be wrong
  • It is a custom about timing, not a prediction about you

The words, one by one

Kaal means a period of time. Rahu is one of the nine grahas (the nine bodies of Vedic astrology: Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Rahu and Ketu). Rahu is a shadow point, not a planet you can see. So “Rahu kaal” is just “the Rahu period of the day”. In Tamil Nadu and Karnataka you will hear Rahu kalam or Rahu kala, and on the news in Hindi it often appears as aaj ka rahu kaal. A panchang is the Indian almanac that lists such daily timings, and almost every printed panchang gives Rahu kaal for the day.

How the window is worked out, step by step

  1. Find sunrise and sunset for your city on that date.
  2. Subtract to get the length of daylight, for example 10 hours 24 minutes.
  3. Divide by eight. Each part is then 78 minutes.
  4. Pick the part for the weekday. Sunday uses the 8th part, Monday the 2nd, Tuesday the 7th, Wednesday the 5th, Thursday the 6th, Friday the 4th and Saturday the 3rd.
  5. Count from sunrise. The 1st part starts at sunrise, the 2nd starts 78 minutes later, and so on.
Worked example: Monday, 7 December 2026 in Delhi

Sunrise 7:01 AM, sunset 5:24 PM. Daylight is about 10 hours 24 minutes, so each part is about 78 minutes. Monday uses the 2nd part, which starts 78 minutes after sunrise and ends 78 minutes after that: Rahu kaal runs from about 8:19 AM to 9:37 AM.

Why it changes from city to city

Because the sun does not rise everywhere at the same minute. On that same Monday the sun rises in Chennai at about 6:19 AM, so Rahu kaal there runs from about 7:44 to 9:10 AM. A person in Delhi who reads Chennai's time, or a printed calendar made for one city, will be off by more than half an hour. It also changes through the year: days are longer in summer, so each part stretches, and sunrise itself drifts by an hour or more between seasons in northern India.

Yamaganda and Gulika kaal

Two other daily windows are cut in the same way, with a different part for each weekday. Yamaganda (Yamagandam) uses the 5th part on Sunday, 4th on Monday, 3rd on Tuesday, 2nd on Wednesday, 1st on Thursday, 7th on Friday and 6th on Saturday. Gulika kaal (Gulikai) uses the 7th, 6th, 5th, 4th, 3rd, 2nd and 1st in the same weekday order. Families who follow the custom often avoid all three for fresh starts, while others follow Rahu kaal alone.

Abhijit muhurta, the window many people look for instead

A muhurta is a slice of the day of about 48 minutes (there are 15 between sunrise and sunset). The 8th one, Abhijit muhurta, sits around local solar noon. Many traditions call it a favourable time to begin a task, and many skip it on Wednesday. Our calculator shows the 24 minutes centred on the middle of your day.

How people actually use it

The usual rule is simple: avoid starting something new in the window. That might mean not signing the agreement for a flat, not collecting a new scooter, or not leaving for a first day at a new job. Things already underway are not stopped. A train you are already on does not halt at Rahu kaal, and everyday routines such as cooking, office work or school carry on. If a time cannot be moved, such as a flight, an exam or a wedding date fixed by others, most people simply go ahead.

What Rahu kaal is not

It is not a forecast. It is the same for you, your neighbour and a stranger in the same city, so it cannot describe your luck, health or money. Nothing in your birth chart changes because the clock enters a window. It is a rhythm for the day, nothing more, and no gemstone, ritual or payment is needed to follow it or to ignore it.

Questions

Is Rahu kaal the same all over India?

No. The rule for the weekday is the same, but sunrise and sunset differ by city and by date, so the clock times differ.

Does Rahu kaal affect something I already started?

The custom is about starting new things. Work already underway carries on.

Which is stronger, Rahu kaal or Abhijit muhurta?

They are separate customs. Abhijit is a favoured window around noon, and Rahu kaal is a window to avoid. If they overlap, families follow their own tradition.
