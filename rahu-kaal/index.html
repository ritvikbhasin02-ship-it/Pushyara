    What is Rahu kaal?

    Rahu kaal (also written Rahu kalam, and in Hindi often asked as aaj ka rahu kaal) is a stretch of about an hour and a quarter, every day, that many Indian families avoid when starting something new: signing a deal, buying a vehicle, beginning a journey or opening a shop. Kaal simply means a period of time. Rahu is one of the nine grahas (planets) of Vedic astrology, a shadow point on the Moon's path that has no body of its own. In this custom the name is just a label for one slot of the day.

    Here is the honest part. Rahu kaal is a daily timing custom. It is not a prediction about you, and nothing in your birth chart changes because of it. It is the same for everyone standing in the same city on the same day. People use it the way they use a lucky time to leave the house: as a habit that gives the day a rhythm.

    How Rahu kaal is calculated

    The day is measured from sunrise to sunset and cut into eight equal parts. Which part is Rahu kaal depends on the weekday:

    WeekdayRahu kaalYamagandaGulika kaal

    Monday2nd part4th6th
    Tuesday7th part3rd5th
    Wednesday5th part2nd4th
    Thursday6th part1st3rd
    Friday4th part7th2nd
    Saturday3rd part6th1st

    The first part starts at sunrise. In winter in Delhi, with sunrise near 7:00 AM and sunset near 5:25 PM, each part is about 78 minutes long. On a Monday the 2nd part runs from about 8:19 to 9:37 AM. That is why a Monday Rahu kaal looks like “7:30 to 9:00” on a simple chart that assumes a 6 AM sunrise, but the real window for your city is a little later in winter and earlier in summer.

    Why Rahu kaal is different in every city

    Because it hangs on your local sunrise and sunset. Take Monday, 7 December 2026. In Delhi the sun rises at 7:00 AM and Rahu kaal runs from about 8:19 to 9:37 AM. In Chennai the sun rises at 6:19 AM, so Rahu kaal starts at about 7:44 AM and ends near 9:10 AM. Two people reading the same weekday chart on their phones, one in Delhi and one in Chennai, get windows that start 35 minutes apart. A chart pasted on a calendar for one city is simply wrong for another, and this is why the tool above asks for your city.

    Yamaganda, Gulika kaal and Abhijit muhurat

    Yamaganda (Yamagandam in the south) and Gulika kaal (Gulikai kalam) are two more one-eighth windows, cut the same way as Rahu kaal but using a different part for each weekday, as in the table above. Families that follow these customs often avoid all three for fresh starts. Others follow only Rahu kaal.

    Abhijit muhurta (searched as abhijit muhurat) works the other way round. A muhurta is one of the 15 daytime slices, each roughly 48 minutes. The 8th, Abhijit, sits around local solar noon, when the sun is highest. We show the 24 minutes centred on the exact middle of your day. Many traditions call it a good window to begin things, and many skip it on Wednesday, so the table above flags Wednesday.

    Which days actually matter for you

    Rahu kaal is the same for everyone in your city. It cannot tell you why your week feels easy and your friend's feels heavy. That comes from your own chart: the planetary period you are running, and where today's planets sit against your Moon sign and your houses. Two people in the same city share a Rahu kaal and still have very different good and heavy days.

    Why does aaj ka Rahu kaal differ from one city to another?

    Because the day is counted from your local sunrise to your local sunset. Cities at different longitudes and latitudes see the sun at different times, so the eighths shift. Delhi and Chennai can differ by half an hour or more on the same weekday.

    Is Rahu kaal a prediction about my life?

    No. It is a daily timing custom, the same for everyone in a city. It comes only from the weekday and the local sunrise and sunset. Nothing in your own chart changes because of it.

    What are Yamaganda and Gulika kaal?

    They are two more one-eighth windows of the day, worked out the same way as Rahu kaal but with a different eighth for each weekday. Many families use them as extra windows to avoid for starting something new.

    What is Abhijit muhurat?

    Abhijit muhurta is the 8th of the 15 daytime muhurtas, the stretch around local solar noon. We show the 24 minutes centred on the midpoint of sunrise and sunset. Many traditions treat it as a good window to begin things, and many skip it on Wednesday.

    Why do some websites show 7:30 to 9:00 for Monday?

    Many simple charts assume sunrise at 6:00 and sunset at 6:00 all year, which gives round numbers. This page uses the real sunrise and sunset for your city and date, so the times are a little different and more exact.
